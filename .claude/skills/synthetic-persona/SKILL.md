# Synthetic Persona — construir y usar una persona sintética

> Traída del segundo cerebro de Isabel
> (`Desktop/Isa's second Brain/08 Skills/synthetic-persona.md`), donde vive la versión
> canónica y sigue actualizándose. Esta copia es para que The Future Designer pueda usarla
> directamente en este repo.

Build a grounded, specific synthetic persona for a project — from gathering inputs to
producing a finished document with a Provenance Card, ready to save in the right folder.

---

## How It Works

This skill has two parts:

**Part 1 — Build the Persona** (Steps 1–8): Create the synthetic persona document from
available research.

**Part 2 — Run the Session** (Steps 9–12): Use the finished persona to conduct a synthetic
interview, screen flow test, or reaction simulation.

You can run both parts in sequence, or jump straight to Part 2 if the persona already
exists.

Ask questions **conversationally, one at a time**. Do NOT use structured option menus for
the intake — open questions produce richer, more honest material. You want to hear how
people actually talk, what they hesitate on, what they're uncertain about. That texture is
the research.

---

## Step 1 — Identify the Project

Ask conversationally:
> "What are we building this persona for? Tell me about the project and who it's for —
> doesn't need to be polished."

Then:
> "What do you need this persona to help you figure out? There's no wrong answer — even
> 'I'm not sure yet' is useful to know."

Anchor the persona to a real decision before moving on.

---

## Step 2 — Understand the Research Available

Ask:
> "What do you already know about this user — or about yourself, if you're the user? It
> doesn't have to be formal research. Notes, hunches, lived experience, things you've
> noticed — all of it counts."

Listen for:
- How confident or uncertain they sound
- Whether they separate fact from assumption
- What they mention first (usually what's most alive for them)

Based on what they share, route to the right path in Step 3. Don't ask them to categorise
it themselves — you read it and decide.

**Research paths:**
- They share real interview notes or lived experience → **Path A**
- They mention public signals (reviews, forums, competitor feedback) → **Path B**
- They reference articles, reports, or general knowledge → **Path C**
- They have almost nothing → **Path D**
- They have a mix → **Path E**

---

### Path A — Real Interview Data / You Are the User

**If they have existing notes or a transcript:** Ask them to paste it. Even rough is fine.
Then proceed to **Step 4**.

**If the user IS the persona (personal project, solo tool, etc.):** Conduct a live
interview. This is not product research — do not ask about features or workflows yet. The
goal is to understand the *person*: how they think, feel, and behave. That understanding is
what makes the persona reusable across future projects.

Ask questions conversationally, one at a time. Let them ramble — the texture of how they
answer is as important as what they say. Listen for:
- Word choices that reveal how they see themselves
- Hesitation or contradiction (signals something real)
- What they mention unprompted (what's most alive for them)
- Emotional charge — where do they light up or go flat?

**The 9-area interview:**

**Area 0 — Personal Details** *(always ask first — makes the persona concrete and
loadable)*
> "Before we start — a few basics so the persona feels like a real person, not a
> description. What's your name and age? Where are you based, and what's your native
> language? What's your educational background, and how many years have you been working
> as a designer? What does your living situation look like — do you live alone, with a
> partner, family?"
>
> Also ask:
> - "When do you do your best work — morning, afternoon, evening?"
> - "Any hobbies or things you do outside work that matter to you?"

---

**Area A — Identity & Chapter** *(+ privacy/boundary)*
> "How would you describe yourself to someone who just met you — not your job title, but
> who you actually are?"
> "What chapter of life are you in right now?"
> "Where do you draw the line between your professional life and your personal life? Do
> you tend to integrate both, or do you keep them in separate, protected compartments?"

---

**Area B — How You Think, Decide & Work** *(philosophy + collaboration + speed)*
> "What does your best work look like? What conditions make it happen?"
> "What's your relationship with structure and organisation — do you build it, resist it,
> crave it?"
> "When you hit something hard or uncertain, what do you actually do? Not what you should
> do — what you actually do."
> "In your day-to-day, what weight do you give intuition vs. hard data when making a
> design decision?"
> "How do you position yourself with disruptive tools — like AI agents or autonomous
> workflows? Embrace them, wait and see, or resist?"
> "When there's a clear disagreement about a project direction, what's your default move
> to get the team aligned?"
> "How do you think the relationship between design, engineering, and product should
> ideally work? What do you value most in that dynamic?"
> "Speed vs. perfection — when it comes to shipping: do you prefer getting something
> imperfect out fast to learn from real data, or do you wait until the experience is
> right?"

---

**Area C — What Drives, Drains & Energises You**
> "What genuinely excites you in your work? Not what you're supposed to be excited about —
> what actually makes you lean in?"
> "What drains you or makes you anxious?"
> "When you're really stressed, what does that look like? What's your coping pattern?"
> "What does success feel like for you — not look like, feel like?"
> "What kind of activities outside work absorb you completely? The ones where you lose
> track of time."
> "How do you balance long-term goals — big plans, life changes — with the intensity of
> day-to-day focus?"

---

**Area D — Relationship with Technology**
> "How do you relate to tools and technology in general? Do you embrace them, feel
> overwhelmed, get excited?"
> "Tell me about a tool you genuinely love. What makes it good?"
> "Tell me about one that frustrated you. What made it bad?"

---

**Area E — Values, Red Lines & Professional Identity**
> "What matters most to you about the way you work?"
> "Who do you want to be at work — what kind of professional?"
> "Is there anything you'd never compromise on?"
> "In any environment — work or personal — what values are non-negotiable for you to feel
> comfortable and motivated?"
> "What kind of dynamics, inefficiencies, or attitudes drain you the fastest or make you
> lose interest in a project?"

---

**Area F — Communication Style & Voice**
*Key for making the persona sound like a real person — not a generic description.*
> "If you had to describe your communication style in three adjectives, what would they
> be?"
> "Do you reach for visual flows and clear diagrams, or do you prefer writing things out in
> detail? Which feels more natural?"
> "When something needs resolving — quick sync call, or async message you can craft
> properly? Which do you default to?"

---

**Area G — The Messy Stuff**
> "What's your biggest blind spot? What would people who know you well say?"
> "What do you tend to avoid, even when you know you shouldn't?"
> "What would make you give up on something?"

---

**Area H — Work Context & Daily Patterns**
*Grounds the persona in how their day actually flows. Essential for designing tools they'll
use at work.*
> "Walk me through the first 20 minutes of your workday. What do you open first, second,
> third — and what are you actually trying to figure out?"
> "What's the thing at work that stresses you most consistently? Not a one-off — the thing
> that's always there."
> "What does a good workday feel like from the inside? What has to be true for it to go
> well?"
> "How do you handle it when too many things are competing for your attention at once?"
> "What's your relationship with meetings — do they help you or fragment you?"
> "How do you stay on top of what other people are doing or waiting on from you?"
> "What's the thing you always forget, lose track of, or have to redo at work?"

---

After the interview, gather all their answers and proceed to **Step 4**.

---

### Path B — Public Signals

Ask:
> "Great. What have you already found, or where should we look? Common sources:
> - App Store / Play Store reviews (2–3 star reviews are most useful)
> - Reddit threads in relevant communities
> - G2, Capterra, or Trustpilot competitor reviews
> - Job postings (they reveal what companies are struggling with)
> - Twitter/X threads around the problem space"

If they haven't collected anything yet, help them identify where to look based on the
product type. Then ask them to paste what they find.

Use this prompt to build the persona from signals:
```
I don't have interview data, but I've collected the following public signals
about [problem space]: [paste reviews / threads / quotes].

Build a synthetic persona grounded in these signals. Flag where you're
inferring versus where the evidence is direct. Use the persona template below.
```

Then proceed to **Step 4**.

---

### Path C — Secondary Research Only

Ask:
> "Can you share what you've found — articles, reports, talks, anything about your user's
> world? Even one well-chosen source helps."

Then use the phantom report approach before building the persona:

```
Before we build a persona, generate a short phantom research brief for
[problem space / product type]. Cover: who the likely users are, what they
struggle with today, what existing solutions fail at, and what would make
them switch. Then use that brief to build a synthetic persona using the
template below.
```

Then proceed to **Step 4**.

---

### Path D — Nothing Yet

Warn clearly:
> "No problem — we can still build something useful. But this will be an **assumption
> persona**, not a research-grounded one. That's okay as a starting point, as long as we
> label it clearly and plan to validate it."

Ask:
> "What do you know or believe about your user? Describe them however you can — even rough
> intuitions help."

Then build using:
```
Based on the following assumptions about the user, build a synthetic persona.
Label every claim as an assumption rather than research-backed.
Flag 2–3 areas where this persona is most likely to be wrong and needs
real research to validate.

Assumptions: [paste what they shared]
```

Add the assumption warning block to the output (see template below).

Then proceed to **Step 4**.

---

### Path E — Mixed Sources

Combine what's available, prioritising real interview data > public signals > secondary
research > assumptions. Note in the Provenance Card what mix was used.

---

## Step 4 — Build the Persona

Synthesise using the research collected. Build a fictional character grounded in
everything gathered — give them a name, a specific situation, and real texture. Avoid
generic descriptions. Every detail should feel earned from the research.

Use this template:

```markdown
# Synthetic Persona — [Name]

## Provenance
- **Source type:** [Live interview / Real interview notes / Public signals / Secondary research / Assumption / Mix]
- **Data points:** [e.g. "Live interview with Isa, ~40 min" or "3 user interviews + App Store reviews"]
- **Collected:** [Month + Year]
- **Gaps:** [Topics where this persona is likely weak or untested]
- **Next validation:** [When or how you'll pressure-test this against real users or situations]

---

## Personal Details
- **Name & age:**
- **Location / native language:**
- **Experience:**
- **Life situation:**
- **Daily rhythm:**
- **Hobbies:**

---

## Who They Are
[2–3 sentences. Name, role, life context, what they're trying to do in the world right now.
Make it specific — not "a designer" but the particular situation they're in.]

## Identity & Chapter
[What chapter of life are they in? How do they see themselves?
What are they building toward?]

**Work/life boundary:** [Do they integrate personal and professional life, or keep strict separation? What does that look like in practice?]

## How They Think, Decide & Work
[Cognitive style — how they process, organise, make decisions. Structure vs. chaos. Uncertainty handling. Known patterns (ADHD, perfectionism, etc.)]

**Intuition vs. data:** [What weight does each get in decisions?]
**Speed vs. perfection:** [Ship fast and iterate, or wait until it's right?]
**Collaboration philosophy:** [How should design/engineering/product work together? What do they value in that dynamic?]
**Conflict management:** [Default move when teams disagree?]
**AI & disruptive tools:** [Embrace, wait and see, or resist?]

## What Drives, Drains & Energises Them
**Drives:**
- [What genuinely excites them — specific, not generic]
- [What makes them feel competent and alive]

**Drains:**
- [What causes anxiety or overwhelm]
- [Their stress pattern in practice]
- [Red line behaviours — what kills motivation fast]

**What success feels like:**
[Not what it looks like — how it feels.]

**Energy & balance:**
[How do they manage competing demands — long-term goals vs. day-to-day intensity?]

**Cognitive escapes:**
[What absorbs them completely outside work?]

## Relationship with Technology
[How they relate to tools. A tool they love and why. One that frustrated them and why. Adoption vs. abandonment triggers.]

## Communication Style & Voice
**In three words:** [adjectives]
**Sync vs async:** [Call or message? When does each apply?]
**Visual vs text:** [Do they reach for diagrams or write it out?]

## Values, Red Lines & Professional Identity
[Core values. Who they want to be professionally. What they'd never compromise on.]

**Red lines:**
- [Dynamics/attitudes that drain them fastest]
- [What makes them lose interest in a project]

## The Messy Stuff
- **The core block:** [The deepest thing that gets in their way — not a skill gap, a belief]
- **Blind spot:** [What they don't see about themselves]
- **Avoidance pattern:** [What they resist even when they know they shouldn't]
- **Quitting trigger:** [What makes them give up]
- **Failure response:** [How they process mistakes — functionally and emotionally]

## Work Context & Daily Patterns
**Physical environment:** [Home/office. Music/silence. Rituals.]
**Morning routine:** [What they open first. What they're trying to figure out.]
**Meetings:** [Do they help or fragment? What makes a meeting worth it?]
**Tracking others:** [How they stay on top of what people are doing or waiting on from them]
**The thing they always forget:** [What they lose track of or have to redo]

## Building Trust
[How they read a new team. What needs to happen for them to feel safe. What signals openness vs. passivity.]

[Does money create pressure? Does it shape decisions or risk tolerance?]

[Short narrative — present tense, from inside their experience. Where friction shows up, where they feel good.]

*[If multiple sessions, label each: "Session 1", "Session 2", etc.]*

*End of all sessions.*

---

## Gap Analysis

*Updated [date].*

**Resolved ✅**
- [Topic covered in this version]

**Still open:**
- [Topic not yet covered — why it matters]
```

## Step 5 — Refine and Push Back

After the first draft, apply the grounding check. Ask Claude:

> "Before we finalise: can each section of this persona answer these three questions?
> 1. What kind of situation is this person in?
> 2. What kind of person are they?
> 3. What would a person like them actually do here?
>
> If any section defaults to generic responses — especially the Frustrations and Behaviours
> sections — rewrite them to be more specific."

Common refinement prompts:
- `"The frustrations feel like a product review — make them sound like a real person in the moment"`
- `"Add a messy coping behaviour — how do they handle this problem today, even if badly?"`
- `"The quote is too polished. Make it something they'd actually say out loud, not write in an email"`
- `"Push back on the trust/abandon triggers — make them more specific to this persona's situation"`

**Watch for:**
- **Mirror effect** — if the persona validates everything you want to build, it's wrong.
  Push Claude to add friction.
- **WEIRD bias** — check if the persona's assumptions skew Western, educated,
  tech-comfortable. Adjust if your actual user doesn't fit that.
- **Behavioral mode-collapse** — if the persona sounds calm about serious frustrations, push
  harder. Ask Claude to respond in the moment, not in retrospect.

---

## Step 6 — Add Assumption Warning (Path D only)

If the persona was built from assumptions, prepend this block below the title:

```markdown
> ⚠️ Assumption Persona — not grounded in real research.
> Built on: [list the key assumptions]
> Needs validation by: [date or milestone]
```

---

## Step 8 — Confirm to the User

Show:
- The persona name and a one-line summary of who they are
- The Provenance Card (source type + known gaps)
- The save path
- Any areas flagged as weak that need real-world validation

If the persona was assumption-based, remind them:
> "This is a hypothesis to build on, not a research finding. The sooner you can check it
> against a real person — even one conversation — the stronger your design decisions will
> be."

# Part 2 — Run the Session

Use the finished persona to conduct a synthetic interview, screen flow test, or design
reaction simulation.

---

## Step 9 — Choose the Session Type

Ask:
> "What are you trying to do in this session?"
>
> 1. **Interview the persona** — ask questions to understand their world, validate
>    assumptions, explore pain points
> 2. **Screen flow test** — walk the persona through screens exported from Figma to see
>    where they'd get stuck, confused, or drop off
> 3. **Reaction simulation** — show them a concept, proposal, or design and get their
>    immediate reaction
> 4. **All of the above in one session**

Everything runs in Claude. For screen flow tests, you'll export frames from Figma and paste
them directly into the chat as images.

---

## Step 10 — Load the Persona Into Claude

Open a **new chat** for the session — don't continue the chat where you built the persona.
Paste the full persona definition at the very top using this framing:

```
For this conversation, you are [Name].

Here is everything about you:
[paste the full persona document — Who They Are, Typical Day, Goals,
Frustrations, Habits, Trust/Abandon triggers, Quote]

Stay in character throughout. If I ask you something you wouldn't
have an opinion on given who you are, say so rather than making
something up. Respond as [Name] would in the moment — not as someone
reflecting calmly in retrospect.

Ready? I'll start the session now.
```

> ⚠️ One chat per persona. Don't mix personas or topics in the same conversation — Claude
> will start averaging them out over time. Start a fresh chat for each session.

---

## Step 11 — Run the Session

### For Synthetic Interviews

Open with a warm-up to get the persona "in character" before asking the real questions:

```
[Name], tell me about your typical [day / week / moment] when it comes to
[the problem space]. Don't describe what you think — describe what actually
happens.
```

Then move into the core questions. Good synthetic interview questions follow the same
rules as real ones — behavioral, not hypothetical:

**Behavioral questions (what they do):**
- "Walk me through the last time you had to [task]. What happened, step by step?"
- "What do you do first when [situation]? Be specific."
- "What's the thing you always forget, lose, or have to redo in this context?"
- "When this goes wrong, what do you do to cope?"

**Pain exploration:**
- "What's the most frustrating part of how you handle this today?"
- "If you could stop doing one thing in this process tomorrow, what would it be?"
- "What does it feel like when [problem moment] happens?"

**Trust and adoption:**
- "If someone gave you a tool that solved [problem], what would make you actually use it
  every day?"
- "What would make you stop using it within a week?"
- "What would you need to see before you'd trust it with [important task]?"

**Grounding check during the session:** If a response feels too calm, too generic, or too
optimistic, push back:
```
That sounds like how you'd describe it in a meeting. How would you actually
describe it out loud to a friend who asked how your day was going?
```

---

### For Screen Flow Tests

Claude can see images — so instead of describing screens in words, export frames directly
from Figma and paste them into the chat one at a time. This is more accurate and faster
than narrating.

**Setup in Figma:**
1. Select the frames you want to test (one screen = one frame)
2. Export as PNG: `File → Export` or right-click the frame → Export
3. Keep them in order so you can paste them in sequence

**Starting the session:**
```
[Name], I'm going to walk you through a new product. I'll show you one
screen at a time. At each step, tell me:
- What you notice first
- What you'd do next
- Anything that feels unclear, missing, or off

Don't think about what you should feel — just react honestly.

Here's the first screen.
[paste the Figma frame as an image]
```

**Advancing through the flow:**

After the persona responds, paste the next screen that would logically follow their
action. You're playing the prototype manually — you decide which screen comes next based
on what they say:

```
You tap [X]. Here's what happens next.
[paste next frame]

What are you noticing?
```

If there's a micro-interaction between screens that isn't captured in the static (a loading
state, a tooltip, an animation), narrate it briefly:
```
A spinner appears for about 2 seconds, then this screen loads.
[paste frame]
```

**What to listen for:**
- Where they hesitate or ask a question before acting (confusion signal)
- Where they go off the intended path — what they tap instead of what you designed
- Where they expect something that isn't there ("I'd expect a back button here")
- Where the labels or copy don't match how they think about the task
- Where they'd give up entirely

**Closing the flow test:**
```
You've reached the end. Looking back at what you just went through —
where did you feel most uncertain? What would need to change for this
to feel natural for someone like you?
```

---

### For Reaction Simulation (Designs, Concepts, Proposals)

Present the stimulus clearly, then ask for an immediate gut reaction before anything
analytical:

```
[Name], I'm going to show you something and I want your first, honest
reaction — not a polished critique, just what comes up.

[Describe the design, concept, or proposal in plain language]

What's your immediate reaction?
```

Then dig into specific dimensions:

```
- What's the first thing you'd do with this?
- What feels off or confusing?
- What would make you trust this more?
- What would make you dismiss it?
- Would you show this to [relevant person in their world]? Why or why not?
```

For B2B or professional contexts, you can bias the feedback toward a specific lens:
```
React to this as you would in your role — what are the operational
concerns, the business case questions, the things your manager would ask?
```

---

## Step 12 — Capture and Synthesise the Output

After the session, ask Claude to summarise while still in character — then step out of it:

```
Still as [Name]: in two sentences, what's the one thing I got right about
your experience, and the one thing that missed the mark?
```

Then ask Claude (no longer in persona):

```
Step out of the persona. Based on this session, summarise:
1. The 3 strongest signals — things the persona consistently flagged as painful or important
2. The 2 biggest surprises — things that came up that weren't expected
3. The 1 design implication — what this session suggests we should prioritise or change
```

Save the output as a session note in `research/personas/` with the `(C)` prefix:

**Filename:** `research/personas/(C) Synthetic Interview — [Name] — [Date].md`

```markdown
# Synthetic Interview — [Name] — [Date]

## Session type
[Interview / Screen flow test / Reaction simulation]

## Stimulus
[What was shown or asked — describe the screens, concept, or questions used]

## Key signals
- [Signal 1]
- [Signal 2]
- [Signal 3]

## Surprises
- [What came up unexpectedly]

## Design implication
[What this session suggests we should change or prioritise]

## Reliability note
[Was the persona grounded in real research or assumption-based? Any known gaps that might affect how much to trust this output?]
```

> ⚠️ Always note that outputs are from a synthetic session — not direct user quotes —
> especially if sharing with stakeholders.
